The Strategy

The book is a leveraged bet on one sentence: AI demand is physically constrained, and the market is mispricing the constraint rather than the demand. Thirteen picks, 82.9% invested, 17.1% cash held back by the single-name cap. Portfolio beta is 2.06 — this is not a defensive book and should not be described as one.

Core (48%, capped at 12% a name). TSM, AVGO, NVDA, ETN. Foundry, custom silicon, and the electrical equipment that energizes the racks. These are the toll-collectors with contracted visibility, and all four still carry double-digit upside to consensus targets. The 12% cap is binding on every one of them, which is the portfolio telling you it wants to be more concentrated than is prudent.

Contrarian (22%). VST, TLN, ISRG, KTOS, CRCL. This is where the edge is. Power scores in the top quartile while trading down on the year — VST is −27% over twelve months against a demand curve that has not bent, and the options market prices a ±24.6% move into its next print. ISRG earns a place for a different reason: implied vol is 0.55x realized, the cheapest optionality in the universe.

Tactical (13%). FSLR, AMD, MU, ARM. High realized vol, sized small deliberately — MU runs 94% annualized, ARM 89%. FSLR doubles as the cleanest squeeze setup in the book: 12.5% of float short with 5.4 days to cover.

Funding sources. Cybersecurity is the lowest-ranked theme and the most crowded consensus long. PANW and S are both AVOID-rated, both overbought into earnings, and both are what pays for the contrarian sleeve. C3.ai is the only Sell-rated consensus name in the universe and trades at 1.67x implied-to-realized vol — the market is charging a fat premium to insure a business the sell side has already given up on.

The risk that actually matters. 67% of invested capital sits in three themes that correlate at 0.65-0.68 with each other. Compute, power, and enterprise software are one trade wearing three hats, and a single hyperscaler capex guide-down breaks all of them together. Cybersecurity and Enterprise Software correlate at 0.80 — the highest pair in the matrix — so the short side is not the diversifier it appears to be either. Size accordingly, and treat the 17.1% cash as thesis insurance rather than laziness.

Method & Limitations

Composite scoring. Secular demand 25%, moat against private disruption 20%, valuation versus mean analyst target 20%, analyst momentum 20%, smart-money flow 15%. PICK ≥ 3.70, AVOID < 3.05. Secular is assigned per theme and is the one deliberately judgmental input.

Prices, technicals, fundamentals. Two years of daily closes from Yahoo's chart endpoint as of 2026-08-14. RSI, moving averages, realized volatility, beta, Sharpe and max drawdown computed locally. Valuation and short-interest fields come from Yahoo's key-statistics module, which mirrors the FINRA bi-monthly consolidated short report — short data is therefore up to two weeks stale by construction.

Options. Chains from CBOE's delayed-quote feed: real bid, ask, open interest, implied vol and greeks. IV/RV compares at-the-money implied vol on the ~30-day expiry against 30-day realized vol. Implied move is the at-the-money straddle on the first expiry after the earnings date. Expiries are selected by open interest so tickets land on liquid monthlies rather than thin weeklies.

13F caveat. Institutional positioning is a 45-day-lagged Q2 2026 snapshot, long US equity only, and was confirmable for 27 of 71 tickers from free sources. It carries the lowest weight in the composite for exactly that reason.

Insider data is Form 4 filing counts and parsed XML from EDGAR; volume conflates open-market sales with grants and 10b5-1 exercises. Activist screen used EDGAR quarterly indexes, since full-text search does not cover filings after December 2024.

Events and IPOs are researched from public reporting and carry a confidence flag and a source link each. Dates marked expected or rumored are not confirmed. Private valuations are last disclosed post-money, press-reported, often stale and preference-laden.
